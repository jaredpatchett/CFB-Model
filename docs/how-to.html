How to use the model

Where the plays are, what the grades mean, and the checks to run before you bet. The model gives you the starting list. You still decide what to bet.

1The quick routine

If you only read one section, read this one.

  1. Open the Bet Card (Edge Board tab, below the Matchup Projector). These are the spread plays. Every play on it is still a bet at the line shown.
  2. For props, open Player Props and look at the Official plays with a 🔥 A. We only bet A's.
  3. Open the Game breakdown for every play before you bet. Check the injuries most of all. The spread model does not adjust for injuries.
  4. Check the line. Bet when a play is first flagged. If the line has moved away from our side, pass.
  5. Bet one flat unit and hit +TRK so it shows up in My Tracker.

3Reading the grades

Spread plays (Bet Card)

  • Win % is the model's chance the play covers. Use it to rank plays, not as a real chance. It has run about 17–27 points too high. Bet Card plays have really hit around 60%, whatever win % they show.
  • High / Mid / Low are win % tiers (77%+, 71–77%, under 71%). High has done best so far, but the sample is small. Treat all three tiers as bets.
  • TRAINED / UPGRADED means the in-season model priced the game. That's what you want. UNTRAINED means preseason numbers only, so be skeptical.
  • Every play has a call: BET add it to your tracker · BET SMALL the line moved our way, so bet half a unit · WAIT our starting QB is questionable, bet only if he plays · PASS the line moved away a point or more, or our QB is newly out. If a pass is already in your tracker, the card gives you a Remove button.

An A is where you start, not a green light

The grade comes from numbers. Numbers can't see a starter ruled out this morning, a backup QB, or a coach who plans to sit guys. An A tells you the play is worth checking. The checks in section 4 (and the walkthrough in section 5) tell you whether to bet it.

4Before you bet: the checklist

About two minutes a play. Run it on every play, A's included.

  • Injuries — the big one. Open the Game breakdown's injury report.
    • Spreads: the model line doesn't adjust for injuries at all. A starting QB who's out or questionable can wipe out the whole edge. Wait for his status, or pass.
    • Props: check the player himself and the guys around him. If a key teammate is out, the player's role can grow or shrink. Rush props adjust when a regular running back is out. Receiving props don't.
    • Look at the time the report was pulled. If news broke after that, it isn't on the site yet.
  • Read the Game breakdown. Does the matchup story agree with the pick? If the breakdown strongly favors the other side, slow down and look for why. Bet smaller or skip it. Never flip to the other side.
  • Line move. Bet at or near the line where the play was flagged.
    • Line moved toward our side (the market agrees): bet it.
    • Line moved away from our side (the market disagrees): pass. Don't bet the other side either.
  • Price. Compare a couple of books and take the best number. Half a point matters, especially around 3 and 7.
  • Tags on the play. Check on a prop means the model is far from the market, which usually means it's missing an injury or a role change. Pass means pass. Big edges on preseason-only (UNTRAINED) games are more likely model error than a gift.
  • Anything the model can't see. Bad weather, a team resting starters, a coaching change, a rivalry or letdown spot. You don't need to dig for these, but if you know one, factor it in.

Why timing matters so much

2024–26 backtest of the Bet Card: +5.8% betting at the opening line and −3.0% at the closing line. Same picks. The only difference is when they were bet.

When the line moved our way, betting at the flag returned +14.7%. Waiting and taking the worse number returned −4.4%. When the line moved away, the play lost −9.6%, and fading it lost the same.

6Betting smart

Flat units

Bet the same amount on every play. A common size is 1–2% of what you've set aside for betting. Don't bet bigger because a play "looks great."

A's only

Bet card spreads and 🔥 A props. B's and C's are there so you can see them, not so you bet them.

Never chase

A losing Saturday is normal. Even winning 6 of every 10 bets, you still lose the other 4. Don't add bets or raise stakes to win it back.

Track everything

Hit +TRK on every bet so your record in My Tracker is real. Judge the model over weeks, not one game.

Don't fade it

If you don't like a play, skip it. Betting against the model's plays has lost money.

Fewer is fine

If the checks knock a card down to 4 bets, bet 4. Passing costs you nothing.

For context: Bet Card spreads are 52-31, +16.28u in My Tracker this season. At $100 a unit, that's +$1,628. That's a strong start, but there will still be losing weekends. Treat it as a long-run grind, not a sure thing.

If it stops being fun

This is supposed to be fun money. Betting should never cost you your rent, your savings, your relationships, or your peace of mind. If you're chasing losses, betting more than you planned, or hiding it from people, take a break and talk to someone.

7When to skip — even an A

  • The starting QB (or the player himself, on a prop) is out, doubtful or questionable and you can't confirm he's playing.
  • The line has moved away from our side since the play was flagged.
  • The play dropped off the Bet Card, or the prop is marked Pass.
  • The game has already kicked off.
  • The Game breakdown and the injury news both point the other way, and you can't find a reason the model would still be right.
  • You're betting to get back losses, or you're over your weekly budget.
